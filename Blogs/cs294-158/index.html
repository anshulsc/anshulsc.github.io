Deep Unsupervised Learning

Study notes from UC Berkeley's CS294-158 (Pieter Abbeel, Wilson Yan, Kevin Frans, Philipp Wu) — autoregressive models, flows, latent variable models, GANs, diffusion and self-supervised learning, one chapter per lecture.

August 2026 · Anshul Singh · ~26 min so far

One chapter per lecture, rewritten from my own raw notes so that each one is readable twice: a short recap at the top for coming back to, and the full derivation underneath for learning from. The course covers the two halves of learning without labels — building models of p(x) that can be sampled and evaluated, and inventing supervised problems out of unlabelled data so a network learns representations for free. The first lecture is the argument for why any of it is worth doing, and it turns out to rest on arithmetic rather than analogy.

Prerequisites: Linear algebra, multivariable calculus, a first course in probability (expectations, distributions, conditioning), and comfort with PyTorch. A little information theory helps but is built up from scratch in Lecture 1.

Motivation & the Landscape
Why learn without labels at all. Hinton's parameter budget done as actual arithmetic — 1014 synapses against 109 seconds of life — LeCun's cake with the bits attached, and the argument that compression, likelihood and understanding are one quantity. Then the map: which generative family bought which property with which cost, and why masked autoencoders have to hide 75% of the image.
