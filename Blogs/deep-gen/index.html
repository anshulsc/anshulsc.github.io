Deep Generative Models Notes

Personal study notes from IISc's E1 286s Deep Generative Models (Prof. Prathosh A. P.) — from probability foundations through GANs and VAEs to the diffusion models behind modern image generation, and how to steer and accelerate them.

August 2026 · Anshul Singh · ~299 min total reading time

My rewritten lecture notes, one chapter per lecture. Everything a generative model does — GPT sampling text, diffusion models painting images — reduces to one problem: estimate an unknown probability distribution from finite samples, then learn to sample from it. The first two lectures build that statement up from scratch and end with the standard training recipe; the next two turn it into a working algorithm, ending with the 2014 GAN derived line by line out of convex duality rather than intuition — and the fifth reads that same objective the opposite way round, as a generator tutored by a classifier, then builds out the practical family: DCGAN, conditional GANs and CycleGAN. The sixth repairs the flat-gradient problem with optimal transport and then turns toward latent variable models, the seventh builds the VAE on top of it, and the eighth puts it to work and then replaces its continuous latent with a discrete codebook. The ninth arrives at denoising diffusion, where the encoder is not learned at all, the tenth makes it steerable and fast, and the eleventh turns to the one family that keeps the exact likelihood — normalizing flows, built entirely out of the change-of-variables formula. More notes (flow matching, modern architectures) will follow as the course progresses.

Prerequisites: Linear algebra (vectors, matrices), basic calculus, a first course in probability (expectations, distributions), and basic Python. Roughly Chapters 2, 3, 5–6 of Goodfellow et al.'s Deep Learning.

Why We Need Probability
Why F = ma works but "pixels → gender" doesn't: three case studies where deterministic math fails. Then the first bricks of probability — measures, sample spaces, probability as a normalized measure, and why a "random variable" is neither random nor a variable. With a loaded die you can roll yourself.
Function Approximation Uncertainty Measures Random Variables
2
Prediction, Sampling & Divergence Minimization
The central problem of ML in one line. σ-algebras done properly, prediction vs. sampling, the three-step divergence minimization recipe, and KL divergence derived from Shannon's axioms — with interactive demos: build your own σ-algebra, watch gradient descent fit a model, and poke at KL's asymmetry.
σ-algebras Conditional Density Divergence Minimization Entropy & KL
3
Adversarial Models & f-Divergences
The generator as a noise-warping network, the f-divergence family (KL, JS, TV from one formula), and the convex-conjugate trick that lets you estimate a divergence from samples alone — the math that makes GANs trainable. With demos for the conjugate geometry, distribution warping, and the divergence family.
GANs f-Divergences Convex Conjugate Variational Bound
4
From f-Divergences to a Trainable GAN
The chapter that proves everything. Why a divergence can't go negative, the Fenchel conjugate and its duality, and then one choice of f that turns the abstract bound into the exact 2014 GAN loss — sigmoid included, and the mysterious log 4 explained. Ends with the optimal discriminator, the two-phase training loop, and a real GAN you can train in the page.
Fenchel Duality Optimal Discriminator Jensen-Shannon GAN Training
5
The Classifier View, Conditional GANs & CycleGAN
The same objective read as a generator tutored by a classifier — which reveals why fooling a frozen critic proves nothing, and why retraining it inside the loop is mandatory. Then the practical half: mode collapse as a stationary point, DCGAN and transpose convolutions, conditioning as a control switch, and CycleGAN's round trip. Sixteen figures and five things to drag.
Mode Collapse DCGAN Conditional GANs CycleGAN
6
Domain Adaptation, Wasserstein GANs & Latent Variable Models
Three movements, in the order the lecture took them. Adversarial training moved down into feature space, so a classifier trained on MNIST survives SVHN. Then the manifold argument for why Jensen-Shannon goes flat, and the earth-mover distance that repairs it — transport plans, Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality, and what the Lipschitz constraint really costs. Then no adversary at all: latent variables, the ELBO, and EM, with a mixture model you can step through by hand.
Domain Adaptation Wasserstein GAN Optimal Transport ELBO & EM
7
Variational Autoencoders
What to do once the posterior is out of reach: train a network to approximate it. The ELBO splits cleanly into reconstruction and regularisation, and both extremes fail in ways you can watch happen. Then the one real obstacle — an expectation over a distribution you are differentiating — with the score-function estimator measured against the reparameterisation trick on a problem whose answer is known exactly.
VAE Reparameterisation Posterior Collapse Gradient Variance
8
Using a VAE: Embeddings, β-VAE & VQ-VAE
What the two halves are actually for once training ends, and the one question worth dwelling on: why does sampling a generic Gaussian work when the decoder only ever saw encoded inputs? Measured, not asserted — 0% of prior draws are decodable without the KL term, over 95% with it. Then β and its two degenerate extremes, and VQ-VAE, which drops the Gaussian for a codebook and pays for it with a biased gradient.
Embeddings β-VAE Posterior Collapse VQ-VAE
9
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models
A VAE with three restrictions imposed — a chain of latents, no bottleneck, and a forward process with no parameters — and every restriction removes a difficulty. The ELBO telescopes into three terms, the denoising posterior comes out in closed form, and after all of it the training objective is: corrupt an image with known noise and ask a network what noise you added. With the forward chain, the posterior, and a reverse sampler you can run.
Diffusion Forward & Reverse Denoising Posterior Noise Prediction
10
Score Matching, Guidance & DDIM
Two results that each give something for nothing. Tweedie's formula reveals the noise predictor was a score estimator all along, so conditioning becomes one line of Bayes — an unconditional model plus a classifier's gradient. And the training loss turns out never to have constrained the sampling path, only the marginals, which buys a fifty-fold speed-up and a deterministic, invertible map from weights you already trained.
Tweedie & Score Classifier Guidance DDIM Time Embedding
11
Normalizing Flows
The family that refuses every trade: exact likelihood, one-pass sampling, plain maximum likelihood with no adversary and no bound. All of it falls out of one conservation law — probability mass is preserved, volume is not — so the density picks up a Jacobian determinant. The whole field is then the search for invertible layers whose determinant you can read off: triangular maps, coupling layers, Glow's LU trick. The bill arrives as a latent that can never be smaller than the image.
